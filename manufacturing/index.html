Manufacturing Performance Analytics

Twelve months of made-up production records from an imaginary packing plant, analysed the way a production engineer would: how much did we make, how much time did we lose, where did it go, and what would improving the biggest loss be worth?

Key performance indicators

OEE–availability × performance × quality
Availability–running time vs planned time
Performance–actual rate vs ideal rate
Quality–good units vs all units made
Plan achievement–made vs planned
Capacity used–of what the lines could make
Energy intensity–kWh per good unit
Downtime–hours lost in the selected range

    Engineering insights

    The Pareto chart tells the main story: changeovers and equipment breakdowns together cause most of the lost hours, so they are where improvement effort pays off first. Chasing the smaller causes would feel busy but move little.

    Line B is the bottleneck, and the data says why: its availability, not its speed or quality. The machines run well when they run; they just stop too often. That points at maintenance and spares, not at operators.

    Energy intensity rises in exactly the months where OEE falls, because idle equipment still draws power while making nothing. Fixing downtime is therefore also an energy-efficiency measure, before buying any new equipment.

    These are observations about a simulated plant. The improvement scenario is a model of two stated changes, not a measurement, and no real cost savings are claimed. On a real plant the same analysis would need verified time and counter data first.

    The SQL behind it

    The records live in a relational SQLite database (manufacturing/data/manufacturing.db): production runs, downtime events, lines and products in separate tables joined by keys. The analysis queries, including the monthly OEE rollup and the Pareto with a window function, are in manufacturing/sql/queries.sql, and data-quality checks (impossible values, broken references, duplicates) run on every build. This public page uses a precomputed export, so no database server is needed to view it.

    -- Downtime Pareto with cumulative share
SELECT reason,
       ROUND(SUM(hours), 1) AS hours,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(hours) / SUM(SUM(hours)) OVER (), 1) AS pct,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(SUM(hours)) OVER (ORDER BY SUM(hours) DESC)
             / SUM(SUM(hours)) OVER (), 1) AS cumulative_pct
FROM downtime_events
WHERE reason != 'None'
GROUP BY reason
ORDER BY hours DESC;
